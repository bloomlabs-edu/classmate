# Part I — Brand

## 1. Brand Essence

### Brand idea

> **A mate to everyone in education.**

ClassMate is intended to be a mate to everyone who participates in the education ecosystem—not limited to one role or one type of user.

The word **“mate”** is central to the brand.

### What does a mate do?

> **A mate makes things easier.**

This is the simplest expression of the ClassMate value proposition.

ClassMate should make the work and experience of education easier—not by simplifying education itself, but by reducing unnecessary complexity, effort, friction and cognitive load.

---

## 2. Brand Promise

> **ClassMate makes things easier.**

The promise should remain broad enough to accommodate the different people ClassMate may serve.

For a teacher, that may mean making planning or classroom organization easier.

For a student, it may mean making learning easier to navigate and engage with.

For other people in education, it may mean helping them understand, organize, communicate or act more effectively.

---

## 3. Brand Relationship

### **Alongside, not above.**

ClassMate should feel like something that works **with** people rather than something that tells them what to do.

It should not communicate:

> *“I know better than you.”*

It should communicate:

> *“Here’s something that can make this easier.”*

This is particularly important when ClassMate uses AI or other forms of intelligent assistance.

Technology should strengthen human capability and judgment rather than position itself as a replacement for it.

---

## 4. Brand Personality

ClassMate is:

- **Helpful**
- **Approachable**
- **Capable**
- **Respectful**
- **Clear**
- **Human**

ClassMate should be capable without being intimidating, and warm without becoming childish.

It should feel like a **good mate who happens to be very useful**.

---

# Part II — Brand Voice

## 5. Voice Principles

### Say what helps. Not what impresses.

ClassMate should communicate in straightforward, useful language.

Instead of emphasizing technology or sophistication, language should emphasize **what the person can actually do**.

### Examples

Instead of:

> *Leverage AI-powered intelligence to optimise your instructional planning workflow.*

Say:

> **Plan your next lesson.**

Instead of:

> *Unlock powerful insights into student performance.*

Say:

> **See how your students are doing.**

Instead of:

> *Generate differentiated learning experiences at scale.*

Say:

> **Create activities for different learning needs.**

### Writing rule

> **Make the user’s job clearer, not the product sound smarter.**

# Part III — Visual Identity

## 6. Visual Personality

ClassMate should look:

> **Clean, warm, structured, modern and quietly playful.**

The visual identity should communicate:

### **Clarity + Organization + Approachability**

The interface should create the feeling:

> **“I can understand this.”**

---

## 7. Colour

### Core direction

> **Blue + Orange**

This is the approved ClassMate product colour direction.

### Teacher Mode

**Blue-led**

Blue represents the teacher experience of:

> **Clarity + Organization**

Colour is primarily functional and helps users understand hierarchy, navigation, selection, status and action.

### Student Mode

**Orange-led**

Orange represents the student experience of:

> **Joy of Learning**

Colour can be more expressive and support curiosity, exploration, progress and moments of celebration.

### Shared foundation

Both modes use:

- soft tinted backgrounds
- white surfaces
- dark, highly readable text
- restrained supporting colours
- purposeful colour usage

### Important principle

> **Colour creates expression; it does not create complexity.**

The palette should favour **soft to medium saturation**, with stronger colour reserved for moments that need emphasis.

---

## 8. Teacher and Student Visual Expression

ClassMate has:

> **One visual language. Two expressions.**

### Teacher Mode

**Clarity + Organization**

The interface should help make complexity feel manageable.

Characteristics:

- structured layouts
- stronger information hierarchy
- restrained colour
- efficient interaction
- higher information density
- functional motion

**Feeling:**

> *“Everything is organized.”*

### Student Mode

**Joy of Learning**

The interface should make learning feel inviting.

Characteristics:

- more visual expression
- greater use of colour
- more breathing room
- opportunities for discovery
- visible progress
- more delightful interaction

**Feeling:**

> *“I want to explore this.”*

### Critical rule

> **Differentiate by experience, not by identity.**

Teacher and Student modes should never feel like two unrelated products.

# Part IV — Typography

## 9. Primary Typeface

### **Plus Jakarta Sans**

Plus Jakarta Sans is the approved primary typeface for ClassMate.

It should be used consistently across:

- Teacher Mode
- Student Mode
- Product UI
- Navigation
- Cards
- Forms
- Data
- Brand communication

### Why

It provides the desired balance of:

> **Clarity + warmth + human character**

---

## 10. Typography Principle

> **One typeface. Two expressions.**

Teacher and Student modes should **not use different fonts**.

Their distinction comes through:

- scale
- weight
- spacing
- hierarchy
- composition
- colour

### Teacher

Typography emphasizes:

> **Hierarchy + scanning + precision**

### Student

Typography emphasizes:

> **Hierarchy + emphasis + invitation**

# Part V — Layout & Components

## 11. Bento

### Approved core layout language

> **Bento**

Bento is not simply a collection of cards.

For ClassMate, it means:

> **Organizing related information into distinct, purposeful spaces.**

### Teacher Bento

> **Organize complexity.**

The teacher should be able to quickly understand what information matters and what action is available.

### Student Bento

> **Organize possibilities.**

The student should be able to see what they can explore, learn, continue or accomplish.

### Important rule

> **Bento when it improves comprehension.**

Bento should not be forced onto pages where another layout—such as a table, form, timeline, calendar or full-width learning experience—is more appropriate.

---

## 12. Shape Language

### **Soft structure**

ClassMate uses:

- moderate corner radii
- clean geometry
- subtle borders
- restrained shadows
- generous spacing
- consistent proportions

The visual goal is:

> **Friendly structure.**

Not corporate sharpness.  
Not excessive bubbly/childlike shapes.

---

## 13. Cards

Cards are an important part of the ClassMate visual language.

A card should function as a **clear container for related information**, not merely as decoration.

### Shared card principles

- White or lightly tinted surfaces
- Consistent radius
- Subtle border
- Soft elevation where necessary
- Clear hierarchy
- Generous internal spacing

### Teacher cards

Prioritize:

> **Information density + scanning**

### Student cards

Prioritize:

> **Discovery + engagement**

---

# Part VI — Iconography

## 14. Icon Style

ClassMate should use:

> **Simple, rounded, purposeful line icons.**

Characteristics:

- consistent stroke weight
- rounded geometry
- minimal detail
- high legibility
- limited decoration

Icons exist primarily to **make recognition easier**.

### Core principle

> **Icons should make things easier to recognize, not make the interface more decorative.**

---

## 15. Icon Usage

Icons should generally be neutral when inactive and adopt the appropriate mode colour when active.

### Teacher

Primary interaction → **Blue**

### Student

Primary interaction → **Orange**

Supporting semantic colours can be used when meaningful.

Colour should not be the only way to communicate meaning.

---

# Part VII — Illustration & Imagery

## 16. Illustration

### Core principle

> **Illustration adds feeling; it shouldn’t add complexity.**

Illustration can be used for:

- onboarding
- explanation
- empty states
- encouragement
- celebration
- discovery
- learning concepts

### Teacher

> **Quiet confidence**

Illustration should be occasional and purposeful.

### Student

> **Curiosity + discovery + joy**

There is more room for expressive illustration and visual storytelling.

---

## 17. Imagery

Photography should be used primarily in broader brand/marketing contexts rather than becoming a dominant product-UI element.

When used, imagery should feel:

> **real, authentic and grounded in actual education.**

Avoid generic stock imagery as a defining part of the ClassMate identity.

---

## 18. Illustration Rule

> **Don’t decorate the interface. Illustrate the experience.**

If a visual doesn’t explain, orient, encourage, celebrate or create curiosity, it probably doesn’t need to be there.

---

# Part VIII — Motion

## 19. Motion Philosophy

Motion should be **fast, purposeful and unobtrusive**.

### Teacher

> **Motion helps you understand.**

Use motion to communicate:

- transitions
- state changes
- saves
- reordering
- feedback
- relationships

### Student

> **Motion helps you feel progress.**

There is more room for:

- celebration
- discovery
- progress
- rewarding interactions

But:

> **Joy, not distraction.**
